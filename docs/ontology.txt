Below is a **structured taxonomy of GeoTIFF raster types** tailored specifically for:

* **Raster algebra (map algebra pipelines)**
* **Raster → vector extraction**
* **Lunar landing site and traverse planning**

This is not just a GIS taxonomy—it is **organized by analytical role in your pipeline**, which is what matters for your application.

---

# 0. Foundational Classification Axes

Before listing types, all rasters have attributes:

### A. Type at each pixel (disjoint)

* **Continuous** (e.g., elevation, illumination fraction)
* **Discrete/categorical** (e.g., hazard class, terrain type)
* **Binary** (e.g., safe/unsafe)

### B. Origin (disjoint)

* **Primary (measured or modeled input)**
* **Derived (computed via raster algebra)**

### C. Functional role (non-disjoint)

* **Measurement**
* **Constraint**
* **Cost**
* **Objective**
* **Mask**
* **Feature**

### C. Mathematical behavior

* Scalar fields (most rasters)
* Vector fields (encoded as multi-band rasters)
* Probability fields
* Cost fields

### D. Data structure

* Single-band vs multi-band (e.g., slope + aspect packed, or vector components) ([Lizardtech][1])

# 1. Primary Physical / Environmental Rasters (Input Layers)

These are **raw or modeled planetary fields**.

## 1.1 Topography & Shape

### 1.1.1 Elevation Models

* **DEM (Digital Elevation Model)** – height of surface ([Wikipedia][2])
* **DTM (bare ground)** (less relevant on Moon unless regolith removal modeled)
* **DSM (includes rocks/boulders if derived from imagery)**

**Role:**

* Base input for almost all derived layers

---

### 1.1.2 Surface Geometry (Direct Measurements)

* Surface normals (Nx, Ny, Nz) (multi-band)
* Roughness (if measured)
* Local slope estimates (from instrument)

---

## 1.2 Reflectance / Optical

* Albedo maps
* Photometrically corrected reflectance
* Multi-band spectral rasters (if available)

**Role:**

* Hazard inference (boulders, regolith type)
* Illumination validation

---

## 1.3 Thermal / Physical Properties

* Surface temperature maps (modeled or measured)
* Thermal inertia
* Volatile probability (e.g., water ice proxies)

---

## 1.4 Ephemeris-dependent Fields (Time-indexed rasters)

* Illumination fraction
* Sun incidence angle
* Shadow masks (binary or fractional)
* Earth visibility (for comms)

**Important:** These are often **time-indexed raster stacks**

---

# 2. First-Order Terrain Derivatives (Local Differential Operators)

Computed directly from DEM via local kernels.

## 2.1 Gradient-Based

### 2.1.1 Slope

* Magnitude of gradient (degrees or %)
* Critical for mobility and hazard ([vector.geospatial.science][3])

### 2.1.2 Aspect

* Direction of slope (0–360°) ([vector.geospatial.science][3])

### 2.1.3 Surface Normal (vector raster)

* Preferred over slope/aspect for physics

---

## 2.2 Curvature-Based

* Profile curvature (along slope)
* پلان curvature (perpendicular)
* Mean curvature
* Gaussian curvature

**Role:**

* Detect ridges, craters, basins

---

## 2.3 Roughness Metrics

* RMS height variation
* Local variance / standard deviation
* Fractal roughness

---

## 2.4 Hillshade (Visualization / proxy)

* Not analytically ideal, but useful for:

  * Feature detection
  * Edge extraction

---

# 3. Second-Order / Neighborhood (Focal) Rasters

Derived using larger spatial context.

## 3.1 Statistical Neighborhood Fields

* Local mean elevation
* Local variance
* Entropy

## 3.2 Morphological Features

* Ridge/valley classification
* Crater detection masks
* Peak prominence

## 3.3 Scale-Space Representations

* Multi-resolution DEM pyramids
* Gaussian-blurred terrain
* Laplacian-of-Gaussian feature maps

---

# 4. Illumination & Energy Rasters (Mission-Critical for Lunar Poles)

## 4.1 Instantaneous Illumination

* Binary sunlit/shadow
* Fractional illumination

## 4.2 Temporal Aggregates

* % time illuminated
* Longest continuous illumination
* Longest darkness duration

## 4.3 Solar Geometry

* Incidence angle
* Horizon angle (azimuth-indexed raster)

---

## 4.4 Shadow Modeling

* Ray-traced shadow rasters
* Soft shadow / partial visibility

---

## 4.5 Energy Availability

* Integrated solar flux
* Power generation potential

---

# 5. Visibility & Line-of-Sight Rasters

## 5.1 Viewshed (Binary / count)

* Visible / not visible from observer
* Accumulated visibility (multi-source)

## 5.2 Connectivity-Constrained Visibility

* LOS-constrained reachable regions
* Useful for:

  * Relay constraints
  * Rover → lander connectivity

---

## 5.3 Horizon Maps

* Max elevation angle vs azimuth
* Critical for polar lighting

---

# 6. Traversability & Hazard Rasters

## 6.1 Mobility Constraints

* Slope threshold mask
* Roughness threshold mask
* Combined mobility classification

## 6.2 Hazard Layers

* Boulder probability
* Crater density
* Slip risk

## 6.3 Composite Traversability Index

* Weighted combination:

  * slope
  * roughness
  * illumination

---

# 7. Cost & Distance Fields (Core for Path Planning)

These are **key map algebra outputs**.

## 7.1 Cost Surfaces

* Cost per meter (time, energy, risk)
* Direction-dependent cost (anisotropic)

## 7.2 Distance Transforms

* Euclidean distance
* Geodesic distance (on DEM)
* Cost distance (least-cost accumulation)

## 7.3 Travel Time Fields

* Derived from cost / speed model

---

## 7.4 Potential Fields

* Attraction (science targets)
* Repulsion (hazards)

---

# 8. Probabilistic & Statistical Rasters

## 8.1 Kriging Outputs (relevant to your work)

* Mean estimate field
* **Kriging variance field** (key for exploration)

## 8.2 Uncertainty Fields

* Measurement uncertainty
* Model uncertainty

## 8.3 Probability Maps

* Ice presence probability
* Hazard likelihood

# 9. Decision / Classification Rasters

## 9.1 Binary Masks

* Safe landing zones
* Traversable regions

## 9.2 Multi-Class Rasters

* Risk categories

## 9.3 Suitability Maps

# 14. How This Maps to Map Algebra

Map algebra operates on raster fields where each cell stores a value and operations are applied cell-by-cell or across neighborhoods ([Wikipedia][4]).

Key categories of operations:

* **Local**: slope, arithmetic combinations
* **Focal**: roughness, curvature
* **Zonal**: region summaries
* **Global**: distance transforms, cost propagation

# 16. Key Insight for Your Architecture

The most useful abstraction is:

> **All rasters are scalar or vector fields over a common grid, and differ only in semantics and operators.**

A clean system design should treat:

* DEM, slope, illumination, cost, kriging variance
  as **first-class typed fields with metadata (units, provenance, uncertainty)**

This aligns directly with your earlier requirement for:

* units
* provenance
* “why generated”
